02 // The Constraint

The reach math, and what it forbids

The governing analysisREAD THIS TAB IF YOU READ ONLY ONE

Your strategy document sets two reach thresholds: reach eighty percent of total addressable market accounts each quarter, and average four to six impressions per reached member per month. Those are the right targets. They are also the constraint that decides the campaign structure, because at six thousand dollars a month they are only satisfiable against one audience, and only if that audience stays whole.

Step one: what six thousand dollars buys

LinkedIn bills top of funnel awareness and video on a cost per thousand impressions basis. For an engineering practitioner audience in North America and Western Europe, thirty to fifty five dollars is the realistic band. Forty is the planning number.

What this rules out

Running both account lists at six thousand dollars a month delivers less than one impression per person per month. That is not a light touch version of the strategy. It is spending six thousand dollars to be forgotten, because a single impression a month produces no recall and no account level engagement signal worth measuring.

The member counts above are estimates and must be replaced. They are built from seventeen hundred companies at a sixty five to eighty percent list match rate, times twenty five to sixty practitioner-titled members per company at fifty or more engineers. Dana can replace the whole column with the real forecast from Campaign Manager in about ten minutes, and that number decides whether the title set needs tightening before launch.

Step three: minimum viable spend per campaign

Your strategy document leaves this open: "Work with Impactable to define what the minimal viable spend should be." Here is the answer and the reasoning behind it.

$1,500 per campaign per month

FIFTY DOLLARS A DAY

This is the floor at which a campaign against a technical practitioner audience reliably exits the learning phase, holds stable delivery, and produces enough impressions per ad to read a creative winner within a four to six week rotation cycle.

Why not the platform minimum

TEN DOLLARS A DAY IS NOT A FLOOR

LinkedIn permits ten dollars a day. Campaigns funded at ten to twenty dollars a day against a competitive audience frequently never exit the learning phase at all. The platform minimum is what is allowed, not what works.

The ceiling this creates

Six thousand dollars divided by fifteen hundred dollars is four. Four live campaigns is the hard ceiling at the current budget, and two is the right number at launch so that each carries three thousand dollars while the audience assumptions are still being validated.

The mechanical result

At twenty four campaigns the daily budget is eight dollars and thirty three cents. LinkedIn's minimum is ten. The fully split structure is not merely inadvisable at this budget, it is rejected by Campaign Manager before it ever reaches an auction.

What splitting costs even when it is affordable

Campaign count is not free even above the floor. Four separate costs land every time a campaign is carved out, and none of them appear in a plan until after launch.

Learning phase, multiplied

Every new campaign restarts optimisation from zero. Four campaigns means four learning periods running concurrently on a budget that can only fund one properly.

Bidding against yourself

Overlapping audiences in separate campaigns compete in the same auction. You raise your own cost per thousand impressions to reach people you were already reaching.

Creative signal fragmented

Five videos across twelve campaigns is sixty ad instances, each with too few impressions to declare a winner. The rotation decision becomes guesswork.

Management overhead

Thirty six campaigns is a weekly maintenance job, not an optimisation job. Time goes to keeping campaigns alive rather than improving the ones that matter.

The reframe: split for control, report for insight

The reason to split by company size or seniority was to see how each segment behaves and to stop budget leaking to the wrong people. Both of those goals are already met without splitting anything.

What you get for free

LINKEDIN DEMOGRAPHICS REPORTING

Every campaign already reports impressions, clicks and engagement broken out by company size, company industry, job seniority, job function and job title. The insight the split was meant to buy is available on day one at zero cost in budget or frequency.

  • Company size breakdown, per campaign
  • Job seniority breakdown, per campaign
  • Company level engagement in Dreamdata against the total addressable market list

What a split is actually for

CONTROL, NOT VISIBILITY

You create a separate campaign when you need to spend differently, bid differently, or show different creative to that group. Not when you want to see a number differently. That is the test every proposed split has to pass.

  • Different budget: yes, split
  • Different creative or offer: yes, split
  • Different report line: no, use the report
The rule going forward

Report first, then split. If the demographics report shows one company size band or one seniority band absorbing disproportionate impression share while under-converting, that is the moment a split has earned its budget. Splitting before the data exists is paying for an answer you already had.

Your own document already argues this

From the Octopus LinkedIn advertising strategy, page one: "Segmenting too early means split budget, multiple campaigns, and supporting assets. By over-segmenting, we also limit our reach and frequency with a split budget. This makes it more difficult to create memorable moments and raise awareness within each segment."

The recommendation in this document is not a departure from the client strategy. It is the client strategy, held to consistently once the budget number is applied to it. Reach first, relevant second is the stated principle, and a three level split at launch inverts it.
Where the splits do belong. Your document already has a home for them. Layer 2 sub-total-addressable-market overlays are defined as tailored campaigns pulled from the master list once the evergreen layer is live and hitting its reach target, funded from growth signals rather than the evergreen budget, and required to have a real proof point for that segment. Company size and seniority splits are Layer 2. They are not being rejected, they are being placed correctly.

The lever

  • Forecast 50,000 to 70,000: Tier A and B, drop Tier C, review at the first gate
  • Forecast over 70,000: Tier A only, and revisit once frequency is proven

Do not layer seniority on top of title targeting

This is the one place where the working plan would have quietly cost real reach, and it is worth being precise about why.

Job title and job seniority are the same attribute measured twice, and the second measurement is unreliable. LinkedIn's seniority field is self-reported and inconsistent. A twelve year DevOps Architect at a bank may sit in the seniority bucket "Senior", while a two year engineer at a startup with a generous title may sit in "Director". Narrowing a title-based audience by seniority applies an unreliable filter on top of a reliable one, and it removes qualified practitioners without removing the people you actually wanted gone.

The concern was right

BUDGET LEAKING TO ENTRY LEVEL

Automated delivery chases the cheapest available impressions, and junior practitioners are consistently the cheapest members in any engineering audience. Left unmanaged, a disproportionate share of a top of funnel budget does land there.

The fix is not a split

TITLE PRECISION PLUS REPORTING

The title set is already doing the work. "Cloud Architect" and "DevOps Manager" are not intern titles. Watch the seniority breakdown in the demographics report for the first six weeks. If one band absorbs disproportionate impression share while under-converting, exclude that band from the existing campaign, which costs nothing, before considering a new campaign, which costs fifteen hundred dollars a month.

One qualifier worth holding onto. The stated goals are marketable audience growth and free tier sign ups. Junior and mid-level practitioners are frequently the people who actually create a free tier account and evaluate the tool. Excluding them outright would optimise against a decision maker funnel that Octopus has deliberately stepped away from. Cap the leakage, do not eliminate the segment.

The sequencing that works

Month one runs five videos against five distinct hooks, which is already a creative test with real signal. Month two takes the two strongest performers and splits copy against them only. You get both answers, in order, instead of neither at once. LinkedIn also weights delivery toward early winners rather than rotating evenly, so a ten ad split is in practice more lopsided than the table above suggests.

Rotation cadence. Every four to six weeks, matching the client plan and the platform reality that creative fatigue on a fixed account list arrives faster than on an open audience. One new video and one new thought leadership unit per month is the sustainable cadence once the first cycle completes.

Reading frequency

THE DIAL, NOT THE SCORE

Below the band means the audience is too large for the budget. The fix is narrowing the title set or raising the budget, never adding campaigns. Above the band with falling engagement means creative fatigue, and the fix is rotation before re-segmentation.

Reading account reach

THE PRIMARY MEASURE

This layer's work shows up at account level and is largely invisible at contact level, because an anonymous practitioner warmed today may convert three months later through a direct visit. Dreamdata company engagement against the total addressable market list is the report that makes it visible.

Red flags and what each one means

Account reach below 50 percent

CLIENT THRESHOLD

The list is too large for the budget. Tighten the title set first, since it is free and reversible, then the account list. Do not solve this by adding campaigns.

Frequency rising, engagement falling

CLIENT THRESHOLD

Creative fatigue. Rotate creative before re-segmenting, or you will solve the wrong problem and lose reach doing it.

Cost per thousand impressions well above $40

IMPACTABLE ADDITION

Every frequency projection in this document breaks. Re-run the math, tighten titles, and check whether Tier C titles are pulling in an unrelated auction pool.

A campaign stuck in learning phase

IMPACTABLE ADDITION

The clearest sign of over-splitting. Consolidate back to fewer, better funded campaigns rather than waiting it out.
