The Headline
  • Five campaigns, not four and not eight. Four video cells carry the engineer band by seniority matrix. Thought Leader Ads run as a fifth campaign across the combined list, undivided, because thought leadership content is identical for every cell and its job is credibility and pool building rather than budget control.   • Every cell has a member target: roughly seven thousand to thirteen thousand. 02 // The Constraint

What eight thousand buys, and how to size every cell

The governing analysisEVERY STRUCTURAL DECISION COMES FROM THIS TAB

Eight thousand dollars at forty dollars per thousand impressions is two hundred thousand impressions a month. Every question about structure is a question about how to divide those impressions so that enough of them land on the same person often enough to be remembered.

The campaign ceiling

Minimum viable spend on a technical practitioner audience is fifteen hundred dollars per campaign per month, the level at which a campaign exits the learning phase and holds delivery. That converts directly into a maximum campaign count.

Why the fourth split never happens

Applying the engineer band and seniority matrix to both formats gives eight campaigns at a thousand dollars each. Every one of them sits below the viable floor. The format split and the audience split cannot both be applied at this budget, so the audience split wins for video and Thought Leader Ads runs undivided.

The cell sizing rule

This is the most useful number in the document, because it converts a budget into an instruction Dana can build against in Campaign Manager. Each video cell carries sixteen hundred dollars, which is forty thousand impressions a month. Divide that by the frequency you want and you get the size the audience has to be.

The instruction

Build every video cell to land between seven thousand and thirteen thousand members, aiming for eight to ten thousand. Cells are not built and then measured. They are built to the number, using the filters, before anything launches.

The formula, so it survives a budget change. Members needed equals campaign budget divided by cost per thousand impressions, times one thousand, divided by target frequency. At sixteen hundred dollars, forty dollars per thousand impressions and a target of five, that is 1,600 / 40 x 1,000 / 5 = 8,000. Re-run it the moment either the budget or the measured cost per thousand impressions changes.

The problem this exposes

Four cells at eight to ten thousand members is a total servable pool of roughly thirty two to forty thousand people. The combined practitioner audience across both lists is nothing like that size.

The decision that has to be made before launch

The budget is fixed at eight thousand and both lists are in play, so the title set is the only variable left. Broad engineering titles, and Software Engineer above all, are what push the combined pool from thirty five thousand to two hundred thousand. They are also the least specific to the problem Octopus solves. Dropping them is not a compromise, it is the decision that makes both lists usable at once.

These member counts are estimates and every one of them should be replaced this week. They are modelled from matched company counts times an assumed practitioner density per company. Dana can pull the real forecast for each of the four cells in Campaign Manager in under half an hour, and those four numbers determine whether the structure below ships as designed or gets merged down. Nothing here should be treated as measured until that happens.

Two speeds, not one

The two bands are not the same size and should not carry the same expectation. Holding both to the four to six frequency target makes a working plan look like a failing one.

Band B: gold fifty-plus

THE FREQUENCY PLAY

Seventeen hundred companies, highest purchase propensity in the total addressable market analysis. Smaller pool, so the budget goes further per person.

  • Target four to six impressions per member per month
  • Measured on account reach and engaged account rate
  • The eighty percent quarterly reach goal applies here

Band A: the remainder

THE DISCOVERY PLAY

Everything in the eighteen thousand list that is not in Band B. Several times larger, so the same budget buys far less depth per person.

  • Expect two to three impressions per member per month
  • Measured on engaged account rate, not reach depth
  • Its output is a shortlist of accounts worth promoting
The promotion path is the point

Band A is not a weaker version of Band B. It is a filter. Accounts that engage there enter the retargeting pools and start receiving the higher frequency treatment regardless of which list they came from. That is how eight thousand dollars covers a wider market than eight thousand dollars should: you buy depth only against accounts that have shown you something first.

Split for control, report for insight

The rule still applies to everything below the second split. Company size, industry and technology stack all arrive free in LinkedIn's demographics report, broken out per campaign. A campaign is created when you need to spend differently against a group, not when you want to see a number differently. The engineer band and the seniority split both pass that test, because you genuinely want to control budget separately for each. A third level would not.
03 // Audience Targeting

Four cells, and exactly how to build each one

Both lists in playENGINEER BAND, THEN SENIORITY

Two splits, four video cells, one undivided Thought Leader Ads campaign. The first split comes from matched list membership and an exclusion. The second comes from a LinkedIn filter. Neither uses company size, because engineer band already proxies it and company size stays in the report.

Naming the two bands accurately

This matters more than it looks, because the obvious label is wrong and it will lead to misreading the results.

BandHow it is builtWhat is actually in it

Band A
The remainder		The 18,000 company matched list, minus the 1,700 company list by exclusionRoughly 16,300 companies. Not simply "ten to fifty engineers." It is silver tier at any engineer count, plus gold tier companies that have ten to fifty engineers.
Do not label Band A "ten to fifty engineers" in Campaign Manager or in reporting. Engineer count is not a LinkedIn filter, it lives in the total addressable market analysis and is baked into which list a company sits on. Band A mixes two different things: silver tier companies of any size, and gold tier companies below the fifty engineer line. If it outperforms Band B you will want to know which of those two groups did it, and a misleading name is how that question becomes unanswerable. Name it REMAINDER, and ask Dana whether the underlying export can flag tier so the demographics read can separate them later.

The seniority cut line: Manager, not Vice President

The instinct to stop budget flowing to the cheapest junior impressions is correct. The proposed cut line is the problem, and it fails for two independent reasons.

Reason one: it is nearly empty

STRUCTURAL

The audience is built from practitioner titles. Someone at Vice President seniority is not titled DevOps Engineer or Cloud Architect, they are titled Vice President of Engineering, which sits on the decision maker list. Layering Vice President and above onto a practitioner title set produces a cell of a few thousand at best, well under the seven thousand member floor, and possibly under the three hundred member platform minimum in Band B.

Reason two: it contradicts the mandate

STRATEGIC

Filling a Vice President cell would mean adding decision maker titles back in. Octopus stated the position plainly: exclusively target practitioners now, not decision makers. A Vice President split quietly rebuilds the layer that was just retired, and does it inside a campaign labelled practitioner.

The line that works

MANAGER AND ABOVE

Manager, Director, Vice President, Chief Suite, Owner, Partner on one side. Entry and Senior on the other. Real mass on both sides, roughly a quarter to three quarters in a typical practitioner audience, and a DevOps Manager or Director of Deployment is unambiguously still a practitioner.

Why it does the job you wanted

BUDGET CONTROL

Automated delivery chases the cheapest impressions, and junior practitioners are always the cheapest members in an engineering audience. Separating Manager and above into its own campaign means its budget is set by you rather than won by the auction. That is the entire benefit of the split, and it arrives at the Manager line just as well as at the Vice President line.

Keep the individual contributor cells funded. The stated goals are marketable audience growth and free tier sign ups, and individual contributors are the people who actually create a free tier account and evaluate the tool. The split exists to stop them absorbing the entire budget by default, not to remove them. Roughly forty percent to Manager and above against a natural distribution nearer twenty five percent is a deliberate over-weight, and it is the right kind of deliberate.

The title set, tiered

This is the lever identified on the previous tab. The tiers below are the mechanism for pulling it.

Tier A: core deployment and platform

KEEP ALWAYS · THE TARGET STATE

Unambiguous, specific to the problem, and the reason the list works at all.

  • DevOps Engineer, DevOps Architect, DevOps Manager, DevOps Specialist, DevOps Consultant
  • Cloud Architect, Cloud Engineer
  • Deployment Engineer, Deployment Lead, Deployment Manager
  • Platform Engineer and Site Reliability Engineer, worth adding if not already present

Tier B: broad engineering

THE PROBLEM · DROP THESE FIRST

Legitimate practitioners, but these are the highest volume titles on the platform and they are what take the combined pool from thirty five thousand to two hundred thousand. They are also the least specific to deployment pain.

  • Software Engineer, the single largest contributor
  • System Engineer
  • Senior Software Engineer and engineering team lead variants

Tier C: wrong industry

AUDIT AND REMOVE

On LinkedIn these skew heavily to fields with no connection to software delivery. Check the forecast breakdown before any of them stay.

  • Project Engineer, predominantly civil, mechanical and construction
  • Deployment Technician, Deployment Coordinator, Deployment Project Manager, which skew to hardware and field rollouts in telecom, medical devices and retail point of sale

The decision

DRIVEN BY THE FORECAST

Pull the forecast for all four cells with Tier A only, then again with Tier A plus B.

  • Tier A alone puts cells in the seven to thirteen thousand band: ship Tier A only
  • Tier A alone leaves cells under seven thousand: add Tier B back selectively, largest cell first
  • Tier C: remove regardless of what the forecast says

Three things that are easy to get wrong here. First, the exclusion in cells three and four is an audience exclusion, not a targeting criterion, and it lives in the exclude row rather than the narrow row. Second, seniority is applied with "Narrow audience further", which is AND logic; adding seniorities inside the same facet is OR logic and broadens rather than narrows. Third, Thought Leader Ads deliberately carry no seniority filter and no band exclusion, because that campaign is the coverage layer across the entire combined list.

Delivery settings, every campaign

Audience Expansion: off

On by default. It delivers beyond the total addressable market lists, which defeats an account layer built from a one hundred and seventy attribute propensity model.

Audience Network: off

On by default. Off-platform impressions inflate reach without producing engagement signal, and engagement signal is what the promotion path runs on.

Brand Awareness, reach optimised

On all four video cells. It optimises the exact metric being reported and spreads impressions across unique members instead of concentrating them, which partly self-corrects an oversized budget on a small cell.

04 // Messaging and Creative

Ten creative units, one ask, one hard deadline

Five video ads plus five Thought Leader AdsINDUSTRY AGNOSTIC, PROBLEM SPECIFIC

The client messaging principle is the right one and it should not be diluted: the problem has to land identically for a bank, a restaurant chain and a hospital group. Deployments are slow, manual, inconsistent and hard to prove, and that is true regardless of what the company builds. Industry-agnostic does not mean generic. It means the pain is named precisely and the vertical is never mentioned.

The four messaging pillars

Change should meet reality early

PILLAR ONE · LIVE NOW

Quality is built by letting a change meet reality early rather than at the end. Highlights ephemeral environments, feature flags, and progressive delivery to tenants and targets.

Delivery does not finish at deploy

PILLAR TWO · LIVE NOW

Continuous delivery finishes when a change is in customers' hands and working as expected, not when it leaves the pipeline. Highlights progressive rollout through feature flags and Kubernetes live object status.

Change should be predictable

PILLAR THREE · LIVE NOW

Not every change needs to hit everyone at once. Platform teams control how change rolls out, teams adopt at their own pace, and critical changes still land everywhere they need to. Highlights templates and policies as governance.

Commercial storytelling

PILLAR FOUR · LOCKED UNTIL 2 DECEMBER

Removing feature tiering, the four thousand dollar first year offer, usage-based pricing. This is the strongest commercial argument in the set and it cannot be used before the December launch. Plan for it, do not leak it.

The sequencing that works

Month one runs five videos against five distinct hooks in every cell, which is already a creative test with real signal once the four cells are aggregated. Month two takes the two strongest performers and splits copy against them only. You get both answers, in order, instead of neither at once. LinkedIn also weights delivery toward early winners rather than rotating evenly, so a ten ad split is in practice more lopsided than the table above suggests.

Rotation cadence. Every four to six weeks, matching the client plan and the platform reality that creative fatigue on a fixed account list arrives faster than on an open audience. One new video and one new thought leadership unit per month is the sustainable cadence once the first cycle completes.

Thought Leader Ads: the dependency that can delay launch

Start this today, not at build time

Thought Leader Ads promote posts from an individual's personal profile, and each named Octonaut has to grant permission inside Campaign Manager before a single one of their posts can be promoted. That approval is a human step with human latency. If it is chased in build week it will be the reason launch slips.

What to line up now

PHASE ZERO ITEM

Named list of Octonauts whose voices carry the pillars. Permission granted by each of them in Campaign Manager. The five posts identified or written. Confirm current objective support at build time, since Thought Leader Ads run on awareness, engagement, video views and website visits rather than the full objective set.

If permissions are not ready

CONTINGENCY, DECIDE IN ADVANCE

Launch the four video cells at two thousand dollars each rather than holding the whole layer. Real delivery data beats a launch waiting on an approval. Drop the video cells back to sixteen hundred and open the fifth campaign the week permissions land.

Two of December

Rebrand goes live 2 DecemberEVERY CREATIVE UNIT SWAPS THAT DAY

The rebrand is a hard calendar gate, not a soft one. Ads carrying the old identity while the destination carries the new one is the single most damaging thing that can happen to this layer, because it breaks trust at exactly the moment a practitioner clicks through.

Assets needed 15 November

Two full weeks before launch day. Creative production, review and upload cannot compress into the final week, and Campaign Manager review adds its own lag.

Strip back early

The new identity is described as significantly more stripped back. Moving current creative in that direction now reduces the size of the December jump without revealing the rebrand or clashing with the current website.

Pillar four unlocks

December 2 is also when commercial storytelling becomes usable. The creative swap and the strongest new message arrive together, which makes it a relaunch moment rather than a maintenance task.

What top of funnel must not say

No demo request

CONFIRMED CLIENT POSITION

Demo requests do not go in front of people in an ad. Free tier is the only ask at this layer. A demo prompt can live downstream in nurture, kept in the background, once someone has signed up.

No free tier prompt to existing customers

SUPPRESSION DEPENDENT

Paying accounts should still see the storytelling layer and should not see the free tier ask. Where a business unit inside a customer has not adopted, adoption messaging is the correct treatment rather than a sign up prompt.

No pricing or packaging

UNTIL 2 DECEMBER

The four thousand dollar first year offer, the removal of feature tiering and usage-based pricing are all locked. No creative in the first cycle can gesture at them.

No vertical language

CORE PRINCIPLE

The moment an ad says "for financial services" it stops being the evergreen layer and becomes an overlay. Overlays are month three and later, and they require a proof asset for that segment.

The gap worth naming

One of the two stated primary goals has no campaign carrying it in month one. Free tier sign ups are covered by the video call to action. Growing the marketable audience, meaning newsletter sign ups, has no vehicle at launch. The client strategy routes newsletter growth through document ad leads, and document ads are gated on confirming which publications to use.

The month one proxy is follows and engagement generated by the Thought Leader Ads, which is a real but weaker form of the same thing. Bringing document ads forward from month two is the fastest way to close it, and the only thing blocking that is a content selection decision.

Why month one is funded evenly. Even funding is not a failure to prioritise, it is the only way the month one comparison is readable. Four cells at identical budget answers a question you cannot currently answer from anything you have: which engineer band and which seniority band actually engages, at equal spend. Reweighting starts month two, and by then it is a decision informed by data rather than an assumption baked in at launch. It is also close to forced, since any meaningful reweighting at $8,000 across five campaigns pushes one of them below the $1,500 floor.

The decision this month. If the remainder band is producing engaged accounts at a materially lower cost than gold, that is a real finding and it should move budget, even though gold is the higher propensity list. Purchase propensity from the total addressable market model is a prediction. Engaged account rate is a measurement. When they disagree, the measurement wins.
